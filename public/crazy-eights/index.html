How to play
  • Two to four players and a standard 52-card deck. With two players everyone gets 7 cards; with three or four, 5 each. The next card is turned up to start the discard pile (an 8 goes to the bottom of the stock instead).
  • On your turn, play one card that matches the top card's suit or its rank. Cards you can play glow.
  • 8s are wild: play one on anything and name the suit the next player must follow.
  • If you can't play, draw from the stock until you can, then play. With Draw one, then pass, you draw a single card, then play or pass. When the stock runs out, the discard pile (all but its top card) is shuffled into a new one; with nothing left to draw, you pass. When drawing is the only thing you can do, it's done for you.
  • The first player to empty their hand wins. With three or four players, the others are ranked by penalty points left in their hands: 8s 50, kings, queens and jacks 10, aces 1, others their number. If nobody can move, or a game goes round in circles for very long, the fewest cards win.
  • Room settings: draw until you can play or draw one; whether you may draw while you hold a card that plays (by default you may not, and since nobody can see your hand, that's checked when the game ends); action cards (a 2 makes the next player take two and miss their turn, a queen skips the next player, an ace reverses the direction; with two players it gives you another turn); who goes first; and a time to move, after which a move is made for you. With friends, the settings of whoever creates the room apply to everyone.
  • Tap a glowing card to play it, or tap the stock to draw. On a keyboard, Tab to your hand, use the arrow keys and press Enter; D draws and P passes. After an 8, pick a suit with the arrow keys and Enter.
  • To play friends, create a room and send the invite link to up to three of them, then press Start game. Against robots, pick 1, 2 or 3 robots and their level. Four colours shows diamonds blue and clubs green, on this device only.
  • Fair play without a dealer: every browser shuffles the deck in turn and proves it did so honestly, and a card can only be read with every player's key, so nobody can peek at another hand or stack the deck. (The one gap: with three or more players, a room creator running a tampered browser could read a hand.) When the game ends, everyone reveals their key and each browser replays the whole game with every card known: you see "Fair play verified" or what didn't add up.
